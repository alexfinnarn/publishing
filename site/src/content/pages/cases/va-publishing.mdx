## The situation

Federal publishing has constraints most commercial CMS work does not.
Accessibility is not a nice-to-have, it is statute. Review chains are real and
they are not going away. And the audience is people who need an answer today,
from an institution they may already find hard to deal with.

The platform itself was Drupal, and Drupal was the right call. The problem was
the distance between an editor deciding something and a reader seeing it.

## What I did

The work was decoupling the read path: Drupal stayed the place content is
authored and governed, JSON:API became the contract, and a Next/React front
end took over delivery. That is a common enough shape. The part that took
judgment was deciding what *not* to decouple, because every seam you add is a
seam that can break at 4pm on a Friday before a holiday weekend.

Alongside it, on the same platform: features across roughly fifteen Drupal
sites, and a Drupal-to-Drupal migration built on the Migrate API.

## What I took from it

Decoupling gets sold as a performance decision. In practice it is an
organizational one — you are choosing which team can move without waiting for
which other team. If the new architecture does not shorten that wait, you have
added infrastructure and changed nothing that matters. I wrote about the
general version of this in
[Intent over Implementation](https://alexfinnarn.github.io/writing/011-cms-intent-over-implementation).
