# Pages (jobs, not routes)

Each line is a job a URL might have later. No paths, no components.

The Site is still for a stranger who might hire. Competence here is direct and
innovative — shown, not explained in a wall of text. The four consultant sites
were useful as a contrast. They are not the bar.

## Required for a competent first version

**Home** — Space. The design leads your eyes around the page. Scrolling can
open a 3D (or other) interactive passage into the rest of the site. First
impression is the job. If they do not want to stay, nothing else matters.

**Problem Sets** — A profile of problems I solve. Deeper than a general work
section. Systems thinking: classes of mess, not a grid of past titles. The
shape is still forming.

**Lesson** — A way to learn something and then contact me about it. It should
be based on the problem sets the visitor is interested in, gained from
impressions. Contact is oriented, not generic. Hear the idea; do not publish a
policy of what I will not do.

## Impression

Feedback from people. Essential to framing and to getting interest. A good
company spots what is not working because it has a loop, not because it
guessed. This solves for the Site and is also the kind of problem a client
may need: conform to the actual audience, not the imagined one.

Depths, not a catalog of widgets to build:

1. Site analytics — did they stay, where did they go.
2. Visitor-initiated clicks on simple things (emoji, easter eggs) — did they
   play.
3. Meaningful form submissions — did they mean it.

Impressions feed Problem Sets (what landed) and Lesson (what to teach next,
and who is ready to talk). Try interactions that teach those things. Do not
cover every possible interaction for its own sake.
