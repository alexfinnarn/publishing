# Questions

Ask these in the piece. The field is a pointer, not homework: you are doing that
kind of work. Types stay in `ontology.md`. Do not add a type because a question
was useful.

## Every piece

| Ask | Field | Owns |
|-----|--------|------|
| What is the claim? | Argumentation | The sentence that could be wrong |
| What would make it false? | Epistemology; argumentation (rebuttal) | Whether you are entitled to assert it |
| Who has to live with it? | Rhetoric (audience) | You tonight / a stranger / a sequential reader |
| Would this stay if nothing is published? | Axiology | Value apart from a site or a job |

## When a topic needs taking apart

Stasis (rhetoric: invention). In this order. Definition is the only step that
is also ontology.

| Ask | Stasis | Also |
|-----|--------|------|
| Did this happen? Does this layer exist? | Fact | |
| What do we call it? What kind of thing is it? | Definition | Ontology |
| Good, bad, or only useful to someone? Who benefited? | Quality | Ethics; sociology of knowledge |
| What should be done? | Policy | |

## When you need a starting-point

| Ask | Field | Owns |
|-----|--------|------|
| What must be true for the rest of this to hold? | First philosophy; *Posterior Analytics* (*archai*) | A principle: assumed, not proved from something more basic |

## Pointers if you want the book

- Stasis: Hermagoras; Quintilian. Short start: Purdue OWL, “Stasis Theory.”
- Argumentation: Toulmin, *The Uses of Argument* (claim, grounds, warrant, rebuttal).
- First principles: Aristotle, *Posterior Analytics* I; opening of the *Metaphysics*.
- Audience: rhetorical situation (exigence, audience, constraints).
- Status / who benefits: sociology of knowledge; Bourdieu on distinction.
