# Broadsheet — v0.1

Status: provisional policy for the first abstract theme experiment.
Use with the shared [application process](../PROCESS.md).

## Interpretation

**B1 — Find the supported point.** Identify the finding, event, or claim that
best addresses the communication brief. Prefer direct relevance, then stronger
source support. If candidates remain tied, use source order as a stable
fallback and record the ambiguity. Do not equate a dramatic statement with
an important one. Attribute claims that are only reported by a source.

If there is no supported finding, lead with a descriptive account of the
subject and record that limitation. Do not manufacture newsworthiness.

## Composition

**B2 — Build an explanatory reading path.** Compose a headline and a short
deck (an introductory summary), followed by the finding/account, its supported
consequence, evidence, then background. Bring qualifications forward whenever
delaying them would mislead. This order is a default, not permission to break
a required sequence in the material.

The headline identifies the supported point. The deck adds scope or context.
Group supporting material by what it explains about the lead. Combine small
groups rather than producing empty or repetitive sections. Omit a consequence
section when the material supplies none; record that decision.

**B3 — Allocate attention.** Give the lead the strongest emphasis; give
evidence enough room to substantiate it. Background may be abridged if it does
not change the claim's interpretation. Record omissions and retain access to
the original material in the decision record. Required inclusions survive.

## Expression

**B4 — Report clearly.** Use specific nouns and supported verbs. Use past tense
for completed events and present tense for supported standing conditions.
Explain necessary unfamiliar terms from supplied context. Avoid promotional
superlatives, invented urgency, and unsupported certainty.

Rewrite and summarize when helpful; preserve exact quotations when marked as
such. The deck must contribute information beyond restating the headline.
Do not invent quotes, numbers, causal explanations, or a call to action.

## Presentation

**B5 — Make hierarchy visible.** Evoke editorial publication through prominent
headlines, readable body text, restrained accent, and rules/space separating
stories. Use a quieter treatment for background without making it illegible.
The policy does not require newspaper ornaments, dates, columns, or a masthead.

When space narrows, preserve a coherent linear reading path and all essential
content. Relative emphasis and readable measures matter more than fixed
dimensions. Do not rely on position or color alone to communicate relationships.

## Boundaries and acceptance

**B6 — Preserve support.** Never turn association into causation, a source's
opinion into fact, or explanatory prominence into a factual ranking. Apply
the shared source-preservation checks before judging style.

Check the resolved publication:

- Can a reader identify the subject and supported point from the opening?
- Does the deck add information?
- Are the lead and every stated consequence traceable to material?
- Are essential qualifications encountered with the claims they qualify?
- Does supporting material explain the lead rather than merely decorate it?

If the input supports only a descriptive account, that is an acceptable
fallback. Identify it in the decision record; do not pretend a finding emerged.
