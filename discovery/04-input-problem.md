created: 2026-08-16

AI has an input problem where traditional deterministic code allowed for repeatable,
classified, structured inputs whereas an AI agent can get anything.

Is the Turing halting problem relevant here?

For example:

> With ChatGPT, I built an AI tool. It begins with a general question: 'what is your
> topic and what is the problem?' **Students respond with a one-word answer**, such as
> 'poverty,' 'crime,' or 'the high cost of education.' The AI pushes back on these. It
> consistently asks, 'what do you mean by x?'

What happens when I respond with a sentence or maybe even a paragraph? 

So the tools and tool calls follow the old deterministic path and become less useful 
as people adapt the process to their own desired input. 

I generally never prompt with only one word or even a sentence and instead provide a 
nuanced prompt that models can now handle with ease. 

I think people who work with AI will have to adapt their tools and probably eliminate 
most tools since the AI agent can simply use documentation, APIs, and sample code on the 
internet just like human developers do.

Not being able to have typed inputs, like in code, means the surface area you can 
build on and share with others grows smaller.
