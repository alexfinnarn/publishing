The last stretch of publishing, you said, usually starts when there is no job and
a site to rebuild. It ends when work arrives. DU is the latest version of that
ending.

Tomorrow, write into this:

**If a job is already there — so you do not need a tutorial series, a portfolio
rebuild, or a framework wave — what is one piece of writing for?**

Not a mission for the whole project. One piece. Something you would still want
in this folder if nobody hired you from it and you never stood up a site.

Write under the question. Ignore the prompt if a better sentence shows up.

---

This made me think of explaining why I stopped writing, which was mainly becoming 
disgusted with the trendy web dev crap and realizing that the writing about fancy JS 
frameworks or new things in any open source thing was foolish for me to do. 

I started seeing how the "you can solve any problem with a layer of indirection" was 
true and that people were building careers off of "artful indirection" vs. the most 
efficient, maintainable solution. 

AI pushed this into overdrive, and here I am trying not to write code and make some 
kind of publishing project by writing things out in markdown files first. 

So even if I could have made money writing about what I or others coded up, I would 
have felt sick knowing people were being decieved from the truth in order for some to 
have higher social status...and I was too late to get any social status in what I 
contributed to. 

I do still like to write, and that is why I want to write again, which could even be 
on coding and tech projects just from this new angle. 

Writing allows me to get further in any idea since I have to defend it for as many 
words as I type. If I don't do any writing, my arguments are not that sharp and I fail 
to grow as much as a person and professional.
