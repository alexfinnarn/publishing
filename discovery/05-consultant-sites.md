These notes are discovery, not drafts. The job is to see how people who already
sell this kind of work turn a systems brain into a sentence a buyer can use.

The lasting list of sites, and how to read them later, lives in
[references/consultant-sites.md](../references/consultant-sites.md). Update that
file when a site moves or you add one.

Open these four. Do not collect twenty.

1. [Autogram](https://autogram.is/)
2. [Lullabot](https://www.lullabot.com/) — Home, then one case study you already
   understand (Georgia or Iowa)
3. [Jacob Kaplan-Moss, Ways I’m available to help](https://jacobian.org/help/)
4. [Hanns Porr, Competent Drupal Consulting](https://www.hporr.com/en/content/competent-drupal-consulting-expert-knowledge-2008)
   — the anti-pattern

For each, write four lines — not a review:

- **Who** they assume is reading
- **What changes** if you hire them
- **Proof** that is checkable
- **Next step** they give you

Then one extra line: *I would / would not want to be hired the way they are hired.*

Write under the four. Ignore the prompt if a better sentence shows up.

A fifth is optional later: [Brain Traffic](https://www.braintraffic.com/) or
[eaton.fyi](https://eaton.fyi). Not another Drupal agency.

I'm not going to follow a four line review because it is too rigid and would not help 
this project. The idea is anti-thectical to emergent design. Instead, I will give a 
free-form response. 

1. Autogram

Nice use of space and contrast. Rather than center aligning the content, they chose to 
have the content towards the left and then top right is the menu, but in a nice visual 
way of each "Our ..." pointing back towards the content like an arrow. Then when you 
scroll down, the menu is in the bottom left with symmetry to the top right menu. Clean,
minimal design I like. 

The conversation, blog pieces are fine, but nothing much better than I see normally.

I will say the services page loses me, and I'd like to see a showcase or case study vs.
a wall of text on https://autogram.is/working/

Overall, I like the direct feel and minimal design but wish they showed me better 
services so I could see myself being their client.

2. Lullabot

Didn't know they only focus on state services...or maybe they don't but that is what I 
see when I go to the homepage.

Once again, getting to the meat of the services and work took several clicks and was 
underwhelming. I don't see the "traffic increased 5x" type of stats or anything 
exciting to make me care about the case studies.

Then I see a resources page with "1275 results". What? I don't want a faceted search 
for a set of articles. 

Idk, I don't really like it for a web agency as I find the design and flow clean, but 
uninspiring or grabbing. 

3. Jacobian.org

First off the Jacobians were crazy so I'm not sure I can like this site...but it is 
simply a blurb and list of posts. 

One post has "Software freedom activists..." and I just can't. What the hell does that 
mean? Software is enslaved now by AI? Everyone is a damn activist these days...so this 
guy clearly does not care about the reader and selling them services or else they 
would have purposeful articles and not balls to the wall activist content. 

"I think vegetarians are mostly right". Lol, animal parts almost came out of my nose 
when I read that...this guy is insane.

Can't even find my way back to the help URL you linked to above from the homepage. Wow 
this site is awfully terrible for a web developer. 

4. Hans Porr

Ow is the first word I have. This site hurst my eyes with the waves and contrast/color 
of font and background. 

Just a wall of text...bad...and sad...

I am not inspired and my eyes hurt.
