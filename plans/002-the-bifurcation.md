# The Bifurcation

Information theory is clear that we only have so much processing power mentally and a finite ability to pay attention to information at any one given time.

Those who took psych 101 in college probably remember the "gorrilla experiment" where subjects were not asked anything about a gorrilla. They were shown a video of people dribbling basketballs and asked to count the total dribbles amongst all the dribblers. In the background, a person in a gorrilla costume walked through the scene. I forget the final numbers, but a majority of people counting dribbles said "what gorrilla?"

Now, if people can literrally miss "the 800 pund gorrilla in the room" when told to focus on counting something, it should be easy for you to see how background videos, sliders, cards sliding in from off-screen, etc can disrupt someone's goal of finding information. 

In the age of AI I see thought leaders insisting that web sites should be fast and accessible, the epitome being well structured, standardized HTML with minial visual flair. No heavy JS interactions, no background videos, no sliders. 

At the same time, people are using libraries like `three.js` to build very creative and interactive 3D scenes.
